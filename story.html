My story

I started coding to solve maths problems. Now my code tracks 10,000 vehicles.

How I became a software engineer, what each step taught me, and what I want to do next.

  1. B.Sc.Tumakuru

    It started in the maths lab.

    During my B.Sc. at Sree Siddaganga College, the lab sessions were where things clicked. We wrote small programs to solve maths problems, and I kept exploring past what the exercise asked for.

    On paper a problem has one answer. In code I could change a number, run it again and watch the answer move. That loop of try, run and see is what pulled me in, and it's still the part of the job I enjoy most.

  2. 2022JSpiders

    Graduated in May, back in a classroom by July.

    I finished my degree in May 2022 and joined the Java Full Stack program at JSpiders that July. I didn't want a gap. I wanted to turn an interest in programs into a skill I could build a career on.

    The course took me from small programs to complete applications: Java on the server, a database underneath and a web front end on top.

  3. 2023ICT Infracon

    Software that people depend on.

    In April 2023 I joined ICT Infracon as an Associate Software Engineer, building platforms for government and education clients with Java, Spring Boot and Angular.

    For the Coffee Board of India I built the eShop, where people order and pay for coffee and India Post delivers it, and the polygon module, where growers add their land survey and register for subsidies. On the Student Achievement Tracking System I worked on what officers use to follow students across Karnataka.

    • Payments have no "almost works".Integrating the SBI gateway and OTP verification taught me to think through every way a step can fail.
    • A slow query is a user's problem.Tuning SQL and stored procedures became work I looked forward to, and it improved database performance by 15%.
    • Shipping is part of the job.I worked in Agile teams from design through to production deployment.

    The work I'm proudest of from those years is the eShop: an order a customer places ends up as a label in a dispatcher's hand and a parcel in the post. Read the Coffee Board case study.

  4. 2025Gnapi Technologies

    From forms and records to data that never stops.

    In November 2025 I moved to Gnapi Technologies to work on VZone, a fleet telematics platform. I wanted harder problems at a larger scale.

    The applications I'd built before mostly wait for someone to click. VZone doesn't wait: about 10,000 vehicles send 20 to 30 thousand GPS events every second, whether or not anyone is watching.

    That changed how I think. I stopped thinking only in requests and started thinking in events: what happens when a message arrives twice, arrives late, or doesn't arrive at all.

    Here I work across the whole stack: Flink jobs in Java, NestJS services, ClickHouse and PostgreSQL, and the Next.js front end. Two pieces I've written up: the Driver Activity report and fuel refuel and drain detection.

  5. NextWhat I'm looking for

    Owning features from the pipeline to the screen.

    I'm open to full-stack and backend roles where I can build systems that matter to the people using them, and grow alongside a strong engineering team.

    I do my best work when I own a feature all the way through: the data coming in, the service in the middle and the screen someone actually uses.
